# Model card - LateShip

> Metrics are **not** written here by hand. They are generated into `models/metadata.json` and `reports/results.md` by `make train` / `make evaluate`. Results from the synthetic demo data are labelled DEMO and say nothing about real Olist performance.

## Purpose
Estimate, at purchase time, the probability that an Olist order will be delivered after its promised delivery date, and map it to a risk level and a suggested operational action.

## Intended use
* Portfolio / research demonstration of leakage-safe tabular ML engineering.
* Ranking orders by delivery risk to prioritise (hypothetical) proactive communication.

## Out-of-scope uses
* Decisions about individual customers or sellers with legal/financial consequences (e.g. penalising a seller).
* Use on marketplaces, periods or logistics regimes other than the training data without re-validation.
* Claims about real delivery improvements: no intervention experiment exists.

## Training data
Olist Brazilian e-commerce public dataset (user-supplied, not redistributed). A synthetic generator (`src/synthetic.py`) provides DEMO data for testing.

## Target
`late_delivery = delivered_customer_date > estimated_delivery_date`, delivered orders only (see `docs/leakage_audit.md`).

## Features
Purchase-time features only: estimated delivery days, seller-customer distance, freight value, product price/weight/volume/category, purchase weekday/month, seller/customer state, item count, freight ratio, point-in-time seller history (order count, late rate, 30-day order volume). Full register: `docs/leakage_audit.md`.

## Excluded features
Delivery timestamps, order status, review data, carrier handoff, approval time, shipping-limit date, target-derived and future information.

## Leakage controls
Allow-list + forbidden list, point-in-time history with strict delivered-before-t rule, perturbation/truncation/parity tests.

## Split methodology
Chronological train/validation/test on purchase timestamp; sparse edge months trimmed; cutoffs inferred from data and stored in `metadata.json`.

## Metrics
Primary: PR-AUC. Also ROC-AUC, Brier score, precision/recall/F1 at the operating threshold, recall at target precision, confusion matrix. Located in `models/metadata.json` (validation, and test after evaluation) and `reports/results.md`.

## Threshold and calibration
Calibration method is chosen on validation (none/sigmoid/isotonic). The operating threshold minimises an **assumed** cost ratio (default FN:FP = 5:1) or meets a target precision; both are assumptions, not Olist facts, and live in `configs/config.yaml`.

## Limitations
* Primary-seller simplification for multi-seller orders; item attributes summed/first-item.
* Zip-prefix median coordinates are approximate.
* Delivered-only labels; cancelled orders are invisible to the model.
* Calibration and thresholds are fit on a single validation window; they can drift.
* SHAP explanations describe the uncalibrated LightGBM margin and association, not causation.
* The API trusts caller-provided seller-history values.

## Monitoring
Offline drift analysis (PSI, KS) between train and test is generated by `make evaluate`. It is not production monitoring.

## Responsible use
Treat outputs as risk estimates. Do not use them to penalise sellers or customers without human review, and re-validate on recent data before any real use.
